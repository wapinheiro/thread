# Thred: Core Philosophy & Design Principles

## 1. The Goal: Maximizing Market GPA

We do not promise students a job. We promise to help them build a
**Market GPA** — a measurable, continuous record of proof that increases
their real-world readiness and credibility.

- **The Theory:** Grounded in [Signaling Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signalling_(economics))
  (economics), which holds that in a market with information asymmetry —
  recruiters can't directly observe what a candidate actually knows —
  high-quality candidates must send *costly signals* to prove their value.
  Claims are cheap. Verified evidence is costly, and therefore credible.
- **The User Mental Model:** "Every artifact I produce adds to a continuous,
  verifiable record — my Market GPA — that speaks for me before I ever
  walk into an interview."

## 2. Market GPA vs. Academic GPA

Students are already optimizing for GPA. A second metric only earns its
place if it's clearly distinct and clearly useful.

- **The Distinction:** Academic GPA measures *academic compliance*
  (test-taking, theoretical grounding). Market GPA measures *market
  readiness* (production-ready competence, verified by evidence).
- **The Trade-off:** Academic GPA gets a student past the initial resume
  screen. Market GPA is what secures the interview and the offer.
- **The Hard Truth:** A 3.5 Academic GPA + a deployed app beats a
  4.0 Academic GPA + nothing to show for it.

## 3. Risk Reduction Is the Product

Most students believe companies hire for "potential." In practice, hiring
is risk-averse — widely cited estimates put the cost of a bad hire at
roughly 30% of that employee's first-year earnings.

Thred's job is to convert a student from a "high risk / unknown" candidate
into a "de-risked / verified" one. The mechanism is **Artifact
verification**: an unverified claim ("I know Python") is risk; a verified
Artifact ("here is a deployed Python application") absorbs that risk.

## 4. Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

Thred only optimizes for variables a student can control.

- **Lagging indicator (output):** Getting a job offer. Not controllable —
  not tracked as "progress."
- **Leading indicators (inputs):** Skill acquisition, artifact creation,
  interview preparation, engagement with component programs. Fully
  controllable — this is what Thred measures and reflects back to the
  student.

Progress in Thred is always measured on inputs. The platform supports
*preparation*, not a guaranteed *result*.

## 5. Scaffolding (Variable Rigor)

Thred balances motivation against credibility across proficiency levels.

- **Low levels:** Low barrier to entry, focused on engagement. Evidence
  can be lightweight.
- **High levels:** High barrier to entry, focused on competence. Evidence
  must be a genuinely costly signal — something that can't be faked
  quickly (a deployed application, a recorded technical walkthrough).

## 6. Soft Skills Are Still Artifacts

Soft skills are not treated as vague or unmeasurable. If a skill matters
for the job, it is produced as an Artifact:

- *Communication* → a recorded explanation of a piece of work.
- *Teamwork* → a written narrative (e.g., STAR method) of a real
  collaboration.
- *Networking* → a logged record of an actual interaction (a coffee chat,
  an alumni event).

## 7. Access to Advanced Guidance

Certain higher-value content — targeted outreach strategy, warm referral
networks, employer-specific guidance — is most useful, and least risky
for the student and the department's reputation, once a student's
fundamentals (resume, professional profile, interview readiness) are
solid.

Rather than a hard, universal lock, this is treated as a **recommendation
gate**: Thred surfaces a clear readiness signal and nudges students
toward strengthening fundamentals first, without blocking access to
component programs (alumni events, clubs) that operate independently of
Thred's internal scoring. See [Scoring Model](SCORING_MODEL.md) for how
readiness is calculated, and [Partner Registry](../ecosystem/PARTNER_REGISTRY.md)
for why component programs are not gated by Market GPA.
