# Strategic Proposal: A Career Ecosystem for BYU CS

**To:** Department Director
**From:** Career Advisor
**Subject:** Scaling Impact — Moving Beyond the 1-on-1 Bottleneck
**Reference:** *The Career Ecosystem Era in Higher Education* (Jeremy Podany)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Computer Science department is facing a "good problem": demand for
career advising has outgrown what a 1-on-1 appointment model can supply.
We've reached the practical limit of a "Service Center" model. To scale
impact without linear headcount growth, we propose moving to a
**Career Ecosystem** model.

This document outlines the current state, the patterns driving demand,
and proposes **Thred** as the connective infrastructure that enables
this ecosystem — an umbrella program that ties together advising,
coursework, alumni engagement, and other department initiatives into one
continuous student journey.

## 2. Current State: The Saturation Point

### A. Evidence of Demand

For the past six months, appointment capacity has been consistently
maxed out, with limited slack and growing wait times for students trying
to get in.

### B. The Organic Growth Engine

Demand has grown through an escalating referral pattern:

1. **Phase 1:** Referrals from academic advisors and student mentors.
2. **Phase 2:** Referrals from faculty.
3. **Phase 3 (current):** Word-of-mouth referrals from students themselves.

**Conclusion:** The advice itself is working; the delivery mechanism
(1-on-1 appointment slots) is what's broken. This isn't solvable by
working harder within the current model.

## 3. Diagnosis: Patterns Behind the Demand

Across many 1-on-1 sessions, five recurring patterns emerge — systemic
issues a single 30-minute meeting can't resolve on its own.

### A. Strategic Blindness

Students tend to treat career planning as a senior-year activity rather
than a multi-year effort, leading to panic in the final semester.

### B. The "Format Fallacy"

Students often believe success comes from resume formatting, when in
reality it comes from verifiable readiness (projects, skills) — they're
optimizing the wrapper, not the product.

### C. Track Indecision

Uncertainty between paths (e.g., frontend vs. ML) often leads to
inaction — students wait for certainty instead of building a broad,
hireable foundation while they decide.

### D. The "Service Center" Mentality

Students often come in for a transactional fix ("review my resume")
rather than a transformational conversation ("help me build a strategy").

## 4. The Proposal: A Career Ecosystem

Jeremy Podany's *The Career Ecosystem Era* argues that higher education
must shift from reactive "Service Centers" to proactive "Ecosystems."

| Feature | Current (Service Center) | Proposed (Ecosystem) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Primary Goal | Satisfaction with 1:1 sessions | 100% student reach via embedded milestones |
| Responsibility | Career center staff only | Shared governance across faculty, staff, alumni |
| Student Access | Opt-in (scarce) | Integrated and unavoidable (abundant) |
| Role of Staff | Counselors with a toolbox | Leaders who empower faculty and partners |
| Capacity | Constrained by headcount | Scalable through shared infrastructure |

### The Role of Thred

Thred is the connective infrastructure for this ecosystem. It offloads
repetitive tasks — track discovery, gap analysis, resource matching — to
software, freeing human advisors to focus on high-leverage work:
emotional support, negotiation strategy, and complex cases that genuinely
need a person.

## 5. Ecosystem Participation (The Feedback Loop)

The ecosystem runs on a feedback loop — every stakeholder both
contributes to and benefits from Thred.

### A. Academic Advisors: The Offload Engine

Thred answers repetitive, general questions ("what can I do with this
degree?"), freeing advisors to focus on graduation requirements and
student well-being. Advisors use an early-warning dashboard to intervene
precisely when students are falling behind.

### B. Faculty: The Insight Engine

Faculty gain visibility into student intent — e.g., "70% of students are
selecting the ML track, but 40% are struggling with the math
prerequisite" — driving data-informed curriculum conversations. Faculty
can also act as **signal validators**: for example, passing a capstone
course can directly contribute to a student's Market GPA.

### C. External Relations: The Market Compass

External relations can pitch corporate partners with concrete numbers
("we have 45 market-ready students with verified cloud skills available
for internships") and, in turn, feed employer demand signals back into
Thred (e.g., "we need more Rust developers") to keep Tracks current.

### D. Department Leadership

Leadership gains real-time readiness reporting to share with university
leadership — a clear read on how many students are becoming market-ready
each term.

## 6. Governance: The Thred Steering Committee

See [Governance](GOVERNANCE.md) for full detail. In brief: RedThread's
original governance model carries forward largely unchanged — a
cross-functional Steering Committee sets the rules; Thred is the tool
that implements them.

### The Philosophy Shift

1. **From Service Provider to Hub:** the Career Center becomes a hub
   that empowers faculty and partners to guide students directly, rather
   than being the sole destination.
2. **Systemic Scalability:** moving from serving the ~20% of students who
   opt in, to reaching the 100% who are enrolled.
3. **Empowering Champions:** Thred gives any staff member — not just
   career advisors — a shared, accurate picture of student readiness, so
   good advice doesn't depend on being a career expert.
