# Thread

**The connective thread through BYU Computer Science career preparation.**

---

## The Metaphor

The name draws on the myth of Ariadne's thread. Before entering the labyrinth,
Theseus is given a single spool of thread — not a map, not a guide who walks
the maze for him, not a weapon to defeat what's inside. Just a way to find his
own path through, and back out again.

We borrow the thread, not the plot. Thred isn't about one hero and one guide;
it's about a role — held at different times by advisors, faculty, and
alumni — of equipping students to navigate on their own, rather than
navigating for them.

## The Concept

The job market feels like a labyrinth to most students: disorienting, without
a clear map, easy to wander in circles. Meanwhile, the department's own
efforts to help — advising, coursework, clubs, alumni programs, milestone
initiatives — are themselves scattered. Each is well-intentioned. None of
them are connected. A student experiences this fragmentation as noise, not
as a path.

What's missing isn't more help. It's a thread that ties the help that
already exists into something continuous and legible.

## The Theme

Thred expresses this concept through a consistent vocabulary and structure —
without requiring a mascot, a persona, or a game skin. A student's path is a
**Journey**. What they're missing is a **Gap**. What they produce as proof is
an **Artifact**. What ties it all together into a single, defensible measure
of readiness is their **Market GPA**. Every component program that joins
Thred inherits this shared vocabulary, so a milestone earned in one program
and a project completed in another both show up as part of the same
continuous line.

## The Program

Thred is the umbrella platform that makes this real: a shared journey
model, a shared data model for tracking skills and evidence, and a registry
that lets other BYU CS initiatives plug in as **component programs** rather
than standalone efforts.

Thred does not promise a job. It promises to help students build a
verifiable, continuous record of their own preparation — and to give the
department a single place to see, support, and connect that preparation
across every program it offers.
